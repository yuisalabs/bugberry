Open the dashboard at your BugBerry address, under `/app`. **All reports** lists the workspace's reports; filter them by type, status, reporter, or project, search them (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd>, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac), and switch between cards and a list. A project's reports are under **Projects**.

## Getting Started

A new account finds a **Getting started** checklist floating in the bottom left corner, open on **All reports**, with five steps:

1. Install the extension.
2. Take your first screenshot.
3. Record your first video.
4. Look at DevTools in a report: open its **Console** or **Network** tab.
5. Share a report with **Copy link**.

Each step ticks itself when you do it, in any order, and stays ticked. The step to do next has a button that starts it. On other pages, and on phones, it waits as a small pill in the same corner: click it to open the list. On a report's page, the tab or button to press next is outlined.

Once the five are done, the checklist turns into bonus missions: invite a teammate, create a project, comment and @mention, connect a tracker, send a report to a tracker, and connect an AI agent. Members who are not admins only see the missions they can do. Collapse the checklist into the pill with the arrow at its top; it goes away for good once every mission is done. Accounts made before the checklist existed don't see it.

## The Report Page

The video or screenshot sits next to a side panel with two tabs. It opens on **DevTools**, with the captured data:

- **Overview:** the notes made during recording, the pages visited, and details about the session, the browser, the screen, and the network.
- **Activity:** your clicks, keys, and page changes.
- **Console** and **Network**, each with a count of what went wrong.

**Details** holds the report's properties and its comments. On screens narrower than 1280 px the panel sits below the report. You can hide the panel, or drag its left edge to make it wider.

<Screenshot src="/screenshots/dashboard/report.png" alt="A report in the dashboard: the recording on the left, and on the right the DevTools tab with Console open, listing Payment failed: card_declined and the failed POST /api/pay requests" />

- Click an event's time, or a note's in the Overview tab, to jump the video to that moment.
- Notes and pauses are marked on the video's timeline.
- While the video plays, the nearest event before the playback position is highlighted, and the list scrolls along.
- Failed requests and errors are flagged, and BugBerry points at the files and lines in the page's code that failed.

## Working on a Report

Open **Details** in the side panel for this part.

- **Status, priority, and assignee** keep track of who is on it.
- **Resolve with link…**, at the bottom of the Status menu, marks the report resolved with the pull request, merge
  request or commit that fixed it. The person who reported it gets a notification and an email. Choosing **Resolved**
  in the menu tells nobody.
- **Fixed in** lists those links. Any member can add or remove one. People outside the workspace never see them, since
  a repository can be private.
- **Comments** discuss it; type `@` to mention a workspace member, who gets a notification.
- **Send to tracker** sends it to one of the project's trackers, such as GitHub Issues or Jira (see [Tracker Integrations](/guide/trackers)).
- The **More** menu moves it into a project and copies it for an AI chat. On phones it also holds the trackers.
- **Copy link** shares it; who can open the link depends on its access level, set from the arrow next to it.
- Pasted into Slack, Discord, WhatsApp or any other app that shows link previews, a **Public** report's link shows its number, title, page, error counts and thumbnail. A link to a **Workspace** or **Internal** report shows a plain BugBerry card that names nothing.

### Formatting

Descriptions and comments are shown as Markdown, on the report page and on its share link:

- Headings, bold, italic, strikethrough, bulleted and numbered lists, task lists, quotes, links, inline code, code
  blocks and tables all work. In a comment, a heading shows as bold text. A task list's boxes show what is done, but
  can't be clicked.
- A single line break stays a line break. Leading spaces and runs of spaces are not kept, so put text that lines up
  with spaces, such as a log or a stack trace, in a code block.
- Links open in a new tab, and only `http`, `https` and `mailto` links work. A link whose text names a different site
  than the one it opens shows the real address. An image shows as a link to it and is never loaded. HTML shows as the
  characters you typed.
- A description written with **Write with AI** and a comment from an AI agent arrive already formatted.
- In a comment, `@` mentions and video times such as `0:52` work inside formatted text, but not inside code.

## Labels

Labels sort reports your own way, such as by team, by area of the app or by kind of bug. They belong to the workspace, so one set works on every project.

- **Labels** in a report's Details shows its labels. **Add label** checks or unchecks one, or creates a new one from what you type. A report takes up to 10.
- **Labels** in the report list's filter row shows reports carrying any of the labels you pick. On phones it's in **Filters**.
- Any member can create labels and put them on reports. Only admins rename, recolor or delete them, under **Labels** in the menu. Deleting one takes it off every report.
- A name is 1 to 40 characters, and "Bug" and "bug" count as the same label. A workspace has up to 100.
- Labels are the team's own notes: people who open a share link never see them.
