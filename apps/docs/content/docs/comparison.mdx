All of these tools turn a problem someone saw into a report with a screenshot or video. They differ in where the data lives, who does the reporting, and what happens to a report afterwards.

They fall into two groups. Jam, Marker.io, and Crikket are built for bug reports that developers act on, like BugBerry. BugHerd, Userback, Usersnap, and Gleap are built for feedback from clients and customers, with bug reports as one part of it.

</Callout>

"Not covered" in the tables means the pages we read don't say either way. It doesn't mean the tool can't do it.

## Where BugBerry Differs

**Your server, your data.** A self-hosted BugBerry keeps reports, videos, and captured requests on your server and in the storage you choose: a local volume, S3, R2, MinIO, or RustFS. Of the tools here, only Crikket can also run on your own server. See [Self-Host](/self-host) and [Privacy and Security](/privacy-security).

**Agents on both ends.** Every BugBerry server runs an MCP server, so Claude, Cursor, VS Code, ChatGPT, and others can read a report's console, network, and failing code, comment on it, and resolve it, with the same access as their person. A coding agent can also record a bug in a browser it drives and file it like any other report. See [AI Agents](/guide/ai-agents) and [Agent Recordings](/guide/agent-recordings).

**The trackers self-hosted teams use.** Besides GitHub Issues, GitLab, Linear, and Jira, BugBerry sends reports to Plane and ActiveCollab, by hand or automatically. See [Tracker Integrations](/guide/trackers).

**Sign-in that fits your company.** On your own server, people sign in with Google, GitHub, GitLab, email codes, or your company's OIDC provider, such as Keycloak, Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, or Authentik. See [Sign-In](/self-host/sign-in).

## When Another Tool Fits Better

**Your customers report the bugs.** A member can send a customer a [request link](/guide/request-links): they record their screen or take a screenshot in a desktop browser, without an account or the extension, but the report has no console or network data, and each link has to be sent by hand. For visitors who should be able to report from the page at any time, a widget is easier: Marker.io, Crikket, BugHerd, Userback, Usersnap, and Gleap offer one. Jam offers Recording Links, which open in the browser like BugBerry's request links.

**Your clients review a website.** BugBerry can invite a client as a [guest](/guide/workspaces-and-access#guests) of one project, to read its reports and comment on them. They can't send reports yet, feedback isn't pinned to page elements, and a client needs an account. BugHerd pins each comment to the element it's about and puts it on a task board, and clients can leave feedback without an account.

**You build a mobile app.** BugBerry works in the browser only. Gleap and Userback have SDKs for iOS and Android.

**You want surveys and feature requests too.** Userback and Gleap collect those alongside bug reports. BugBerry is for bug reports only.

**Your team lives in support and chat tools.** Jam, Userback, Usersnap, and BugHerd connect to Slack and support desks such as Intercom, Zendesk, or ServiceNow, and Gleap has its own in-app chat. BugBerry connects to issue trackers and AI agents only.

**You don't want to run a server.** Use the [BugBerry cloud](https://bugberry.yuisalabs.com), or a hosted tool. Running BugBerry yourself means looking after Docker, a database, and backups. See [Operations](/self-host/operations).
